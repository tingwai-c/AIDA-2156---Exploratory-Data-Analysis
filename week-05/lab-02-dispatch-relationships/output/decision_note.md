The scatter plot reveals a clear positive relationship between task difficulty ratings and technician labor hours. Overall, tasks rated as more difficult require a greater investment of technician time. This pattern holds true across all three regions; consequently, factoring in the service region does not alter the general interpretation of the relationship between these two variables.
However, there are indeed variations across regions: the Central region records the highest average labor hours, followed by the South, while the North shows the lowest. Additionally, an outlier appears in the Northern region data; for a task with a difficulty rating of 5, the technician labor hours were significantly higher than those for most other tasks with the same rating.
Potential next steps involve examining whether job type helps explain variations in technician labor hours among tasks with similar difficulty ratings and across different service regions.
The primary limitation of this analysis is that it is based on a small-scale, synthetic dataset. The observed relationship indicates a correlation only; it does not prove that higher task difficulty, different service regions, or any other factor is the direct cause of increased technician labor hours.
